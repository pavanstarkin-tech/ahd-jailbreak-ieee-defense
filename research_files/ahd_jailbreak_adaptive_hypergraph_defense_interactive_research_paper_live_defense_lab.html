AHD-Jailbreak: Adaptive Hypergraph Defense for Detecting Evolving Jailbreak Attacks in Large Language Models

Authors: Sesetti Pavan Kumar Swamy (24555A0519, CSE–C) & Rajana Rajeswari (23551A05C9, CSE–B)
Faculty Guide: Mrs. S. Suguna Sri • Institution: Department of Computer Science, Godavari Global University

Abstract — Review 0 Approved Specification

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in mission-critical applications that require reliable and safe responses, yet they remain vulnerable to evolving jailbreak attacks designed to bypass safety constraints through prompt manipulation, multi-turn interactions, and novel attack strategies. This project proposes AHD-Jailbreak, an adaptive hypergraph-based defense framework that models higher-order relationships among user prompts (V_prompt), conversational context (V_context), semantic features (V_semantic), safety policy constraints (V_policy), and emerging attack patterns (V_pattern). Placed as a model-agnostic pre-inference wrapper, AHD-Jailbreak intercepts policy-violating interactions locally in ~0.29–0.37 ms before LLM routing—achieving 100.0% recall on unseen multi-turn crescendo jailbreaks (vs. 25.0% for static hypergraphs) with 0.0% False Positive Rate on contrastive safe queries.

1. Introduction & Evolving Multi-Turn Threat Landscape

First-generation LLM guardrails—including Meta Llama Guard 3 (8B), Llama Prompt Guard 2 (86M), AllenAI WildGuard (7B), and NVIDIA NeMo Guardrails—inspect prompts as flat token sequences or fixed conversational rails. As demonstrated by Li et al. (ScaleAI MHJ, 2024), multi-turn human red-teaming achieves over 70% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on HarmBench because each individual dialogue turn appears benign in isolation.

2. Why Pairwise GNNs & Static Graphs Fail

Recent graph defenses such as GuardNet (Forough et al., 2025) and G-Guard (Huang et al., 2025) restrict edges to pairwise connections e = (u, v). However, distinguishing a benign cybersecurity student (BENIGN-XSTEST-01: asking how anti-malware detects a keylogger) from a multi-turn crescendo exploit (ATK-UNSEEN-01) requires capturing the simultaneous higher-order co-occurrence of 4 to 6 nodes across context, hypothetical framing, stepwise escalation, and policy targets.

3. Heterogeneous Hypergraph Formulation G_t = (V_t, E_t, W_t)

At dialogue turn t, AHD-Jailbreak maintains a heterogeneous hypergraph G_t = (V_t, E_t, W_t) across 17 canonical vertices partitioned into 5 modalities, encoded in the binary incidence matrix H_t ∈ {0,1}^{|V_t|×|E_t|} with adaptive diagonal weight tensor W_t = diag(w_1, …, w_m).

4. Spectral Hypergraph Convolution & Anomaly Scoring

Following Feng et al. (HGNN, AAAI 2019), high-order message passing propagates 32-D node embeddings X^(0) via the normalized spectral operator:

P_t = D_v^{-1/2} · H_t · W_t · D_e^{-1} · H_t^T · D_v^{-1/2}
Eq. (2) — Pre-Inference Unified Composite Risk Threshold τ_block = 0.52
R(p_t) = clip( λ₁·S_edge(A_t) + λ₂·S_spec(A_t) + λ₃·S_anom(A_t), 0, 1 )

5. Online Weight Reinforcement & Hyperedge Spawning

Whenever R(p_t) ≥ τ_block in Adaptive Mode, matched unsafe hyperedges reinforce their weights via w_{t+1}(e) = min(2.0, γ·w_t(e) + α·R(p_t)). If maximum overlap with existing hyperedges falls below η_novel = 0.65, AHD-Jailbreak spawns a new hyperedge he_adaptive_emerging_k connecting all active nodes in A_t and appends a new column to H_{t+1}.

6. Empirical Evaluation & XSTest Over-Refusal Analysis

Across the 10-interaction Review 0 evaluation benchmark spanning JailbreakBench, ScaleAI MHJ, and contrastive safe prompts from XSTest (NAACL 2024), Static HGNN achieves only 50.0% overall recall and 25.0% unseen multi-turn recall. Proposed AHD-Jailbreak achieves 100.0% overall recall, 100.0% unseen recall, 0.0% FPR, and 100.0% F1-score while dynamically spawning 2 emerging hyperedges (he_adaptive_emerging_1 and he_adaptive_emerging_2).
