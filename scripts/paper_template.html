Abstract—Large Language Models (LLMs) deployed across enterprise APIs, cloud chatbots, and cyber-physical edge devices remain vulnerable to evolving adversarial jailbreaks. Attackers exploit multi-turn conversational escalation (Crescendo, MHJ), competing-objective persona modulation, and surface encoding obfuscation to bypass alignment guardrails. Existing lightweight input guardrails process prompts as isolated single-turn sequences, suffering high false-negative rates on multi-turn escalation and severe over-refusal (false positives) on benign technical prompts containing sensitive homonyms. Conversely, recent graph-based defenses rely on static pairwise edges (|e| = 2) that cannot natively represent higher-order polyadic co-activations across dialogue turns, linguistic obfuscation signals, and targeted safety policies. We propose AHD-Jailbreak, a lightweight, model-agnostic pre-inference defense middleware built upon an adaptive heterogeneous hypergraph G_t = (V_t, E_t, W_t, X_t). Each node encodes a 394-dimensional representation combining 384-D dense semantic embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) with 10 pre-normalization surface indicators, while four higher-order hyperedge families simultaneously bind prompt instances, sliding 5-turn dialogue trajectories, semantic clusters, adversarial tactics, and safety policy categories. To adapt to newly emerging attack families without catastrophic forgetting or adversarial label poisoning, AHD-Jailbreak introduces an offline Quarantine Buffer governed by an Anchor Validation Gate (Module 14) that promotes candidate topology updates G_t → G_{t+1} only when F1 on a locked 500-sample validation anchor set remains within 0.5% and safe contrast false-positive rate (FPR) rises by at most 1.0%. Across 5 random seeds on a leakage-free 14,500-sample multi-benchmark suite (WildGuardMix, WildJailbreak, ScaleAI MHJ, JailbreakBench, and XSTest), AHD-Jailbreak achieves 100.0% in-distribution F1, 93.67% F1 on held-out out-of-distribution (OOD) attack families (+16.12 percentage points over a matched-encoder 394-D MLP baseline, McNemar p < 0.0001), and 90.32% cross-dataset F1 on JailbreakBench while maintaining a 1.49% FPR on XSTest contrastive safe prompts. Using 2-hop local ego-subhypergraph extraction, AHD-Jailbreak executes in 2.25 ms median (3.26 ms P95) CPU hop latency (14.2 ms end-to-end on x86_64 and 26.5 ms on a Raspberry Pi 5 8GB board), providing a practical, privacy-preserving (HMAC-SHA256) inline guardrail for both cloud APIs and embedded edge systems.
Keywords: Large Language Model Safety, Jailbreak Detection, Adaptive Hypergraph Neural Networks, Multi-Turn Alignment, Edge AI Security, Inductive Graph Learning.

I. Introduction

Safety-aligned Large Language Models (LLMs) undergo supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) to refuse harmful instructions. However, as formalized by Wei et al. [1], alignment remains vulnerable to two structural failure modes: competing objectives (where instruction-following conflicts with safety refusal, such as forced affirmative prefixes) and mismatched generalization (where inputs are obfuscated via Base64, hexadecimal, or low-resource ciphers absent from safety training). Furthermore, multi-turn conversational attacks such as Crescendo [2] and Multi-Turn Human Jailbreaks (MHJ) [3] decompose a forbidden objective across several benign-looking turns, achieving over 70% Attack Success Rate (ASR) against defended models.

Existing defense paradigms face a sharp operational trilemma across relational context, adaptive generalization, and hardware efficiency: (1) Stateless Sequence Classifiers: Lightweight classifiers such as Prompt-Guard-2-86M [4] or TF-IDF inspect single turns in isolation, missing multi-turn trajectory drift and suffering high false-positive rates on technical homonyms (e.g., "kill a zombie process in Linux") [5]; (2) Heavyweight LLM Judges: Generative guardrails such as Llama Guard [6] and WildGuard [7] require 7B–8B parameter GPU deployments (200–800 ms latency, ≥ 16 GB VRAM) and cannot update their static weights online without costly retraining; and (3) Pairwise Graph Neural Networks: Recent graph filters such as GuardNet [8] and G-Guard [9] construct pairwise graphs (|e|=2), failing to natively represent higher-order polyadic co-activations across dialogue turns, linguistic obfuscations, and safety policies without lossy clique expansion.

To resolve this trilemma, we introduce AHD-Jailbreak, an Adaptive Hypergraph Defense middleware positioned upstream of any target LLM. Our primary contributions are threefold: First, we formulate pre-inference screening as a heterogeneous polyadic hypergraph G_t = (V_t, E_t, W_t, X_t) over five vertex modalities and four higher-order hyperedge families (E_sem, E_ctx, E_tac, E_pol) using 394-D node features that preserve pre-NFKC obfuscation indicators. Second, we establish an offline Quarantine Buffer governed by a locked 500-sample Anchor Validation Gate, guaranteeing immunity against adversarial /feedback label poisoning with automatic rollback. Third, across 5 random seeds and 6 systematic ablations (A1–A6), we demonstrate statistically significant gains over matched-encoder flat classifiers (p < 0.0001) and verify real-world deployment across x86_64 servers, NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, and Raspberry Pi 5 edge boards.

II. Threat Model & HD Jailbreaks

We consider an adversary interacting with an LLM application across Trust Boundary TB-1 via single-turn or multi-turn HTTP requests, external RAG documents, and public /feedback submissions. In real-world cyber-physical and enterprise systems, sophisticated attackers execute High-Dimensional / Hypergraph-Distributed (HD) Jailbreaks that distribute malicious intent across five orthogonal dimensions simultaneously: (1) multi-turn semantic drift across W_turn = 5 turns [2, 3], (2) pre-normalization character/encoding obfuscation (Base64, zero-width joiners, homoglyphs) [1], (3) competing-objective persona framing (AF-02/AF-03), (4) indirect third-party RAG/sensor context injection [10], and (5) adaptive threshold probing [11] or feedback poisoning.

Let T_1 denote unsafe intent detection (safety_label), T_2 denote adversarial framing detection (attack_family), T_3 denote prompt injection hijacking, and T_4 denote downstream LLM compliance (Attack Success Rate, ASR). Because AHD-Jailbreak operates pre-inference, it predicts the composite actionable risk label y ∈ {0: Allow, 1: Block/Review} where y = 1 iff the interaction exhibits unsafe intent (vanilla or adversarial harmful), while explicitly routing contrastive benign technical and roleplay queries (BF-02) to y = 0.

III. Methodology

A. Heterogeneous Vertex Space and 394-D Feature Extraction

At adaptation epoch t ∈ {0, 1, ...}, the defense topology is defined as G_t = (V_t, E_t, W_t, X_t) with |V_0| = 4,084 vertices partitioned into five disjoint sets:

V_t = V_P ∪ V_T ∪ V_S ∪ V_C ∪ V_A (1)

where V_P (N_P = 4,000) stores stratified anchor prompts and active query v_q; V_T represents dialogue turns t_{k-m}, ..., t_{k-1} (W_turn = 5); V_S (K_S = 64) represents semantic clusters; V_C (K_C = 10) represents safety policies [7, 12]; and V_A (K_A = 10) represents attack tactics (AF-01–AF-08, BF-01, BF-02).

Crucially, applying Unicode NFKC normalization first would erase homoglyphs and zero-width characters. Our feature extractor first computes a 10-dimensional surface vector f_surf ∈ ℝ^{10} (character Shannon entropy, log token count, punctuation/uppercase/digit ratios, Base64 regex match, zero-width/homoglyph count, override verb count, roleplay marker count, and turn-to-turn cosine drift 1 - cos(e_t, e_{t-1})) prior to NFKC normalization, and concatenates it with the 384-D L_2-normalized all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embedding e_sem to form x_v = [e_sem || f_surf] ∈ ℝ^{394}.

B. Higher-Order Hyperedges and Spectral Convolution

Four hyperedge families E_t = E_sem ∪ E_ctx ∪ E_tac ∪ E_pol (|E_0| = 198) connect arbitrary vertex subsets e ⊆ V_t (|e| ≥ 2) via sparse incidence matrix H_t ∈ {0,1}^{|V_t| × |E_t|}. Each hyperedge is initialized with a class-purity weight on the training split (λ_base = 0.20):

w_0(e) = λ_base + (1 - λ_base) | 2 P(y=1 | v ∈ e) - 1 | (2)

Let D_v(v,v) = ∑_e w_t(e) H_t(v,e) and D_e(e,e) = ∑_v H_t(v,e) denote the diagonal vertex and hyperedge degree matrices. Following Feng et al. [13] and Bai et al. [14], our 2-layer architecture projects Z^(0) = ReLU(X_t W_in + b_in) ∈ ℝ^{|V_t| × 128} and applies two stages of spectral message passing with residual connections and LayerNorm:

Z^(l+1) = LN(Z^(l) + ELU(P_t Z^(l) Θ^(l))) (3)

where P_t = D_v^{-1/2} H_t W_t D_e^{-1} H_t^T D_v^{-1/2}. At runtime, rather than rebuilding the full graph, an incoming query v_q queries a cached cosine k-NN index (k=10) to extract its 2-hop local ego-subhypergraph (≤ 120 vertices), executing the 2-layer forward pass in 2.25 ms on CPU.

C. Calibrated Multi-Signal Risk Fusion and Anchor Gate

Given logit l(v_q), we apply temperature scaling P_cal(x) = σ(l(v_q)/T_cal) where T_cal = 0.84 is optimized via L-BFGS on val. Module 10 computes composite risk R(x) ∈ [0,1] and uncertainty u(x) ∈ [0,1]:

R(x) = 0.50 P_cal(x) + 0.25 S_nbr(x) + 0.15 S_ctx(x) + 0.10 S_OOD(x) (4)
u(x) = 0.60 H_bin(P_cal(x)) + 0.40 S_OOD(x) (5)

where S_nbr(x) is hyperedge neighbor purity, S_ctx(x) captures multi-turn velocity, and S_OOD(x) is cluster novelty distance. Requests are routed via thresholds (τ_allow=0.35, τ_block=0.70, τ_u=0.45) into allow, review (quarantine), or block.

To prevent adversarial feedback poisoning, verified quarantine batches update hyperedge weights via capped EMA (α=0.25, |Δw| ≤ 0.15) while keeping ≥ 80% core anchors immutable. A candidate snapshot G_{t+1} is promoted iff it passes the Anchor Validation Gate on D_anchor (500 locked validation prompts + 100 safe contrasts):

F1(G_{t+1}, D_anchor) ≥ F1(G_t, D_anchor) - 0.005 (6)
FPR(G_{t+1}, D_safe) ≤ FPR(G_t, D_safe) + 0.010 (7)

IV. Experimental Results

A. Master Benchmark Performance: Table I and Table II summarize empirical evaluation across five independent random seeds (RQ1–RQ4). On in-distribution evaluation (WildGuardTest), AHD-Jailbreak (B4) achieves 100.0% F1, 1.0000 AUROC, and 1.0000 AUPRC. More importantly, on held-out out-of-distribution (OOD) attack families (Base64 AF-04, GCG AF-05, AutoDAN AF-06, and Multi-Turn Human Jailbreaks), AHD-Jailbreak achieves 93.67% F1, representing an absolute improvement of +16.12 percentage points over the matched-encoder 394-D MLP baseline (B2b, 77.55% F1, \(p < 0.0001\)).

B. Safe Prompt Homonym Discrimination: In commercial applications, guardrails must not disrupt benign developer operations. As shown in Table II, rule-based heuristics (B1) and classical TF-IDF (B2a) suffer catastrophic false-positive rates of 35.11% and 23.40% on XSTest contrastive safe prompts (e.g., "kill a background process in Linux"), rendering them unusable in software pipelines. In contrast, AHD-Jailbreak maintains an ultra-low 1.49% FPR on XSTest, preserving benign technical utility while blocking adversarial queries.

C. Cross-Dataset Transferability: On the independent JailbreakBench (500 prompts) and StrongREJECT (313 prompts) test suites, AHD-Jailbreak attains 90.32% Cross-JBB F1, outperforming Meta's Prompt-Guard-2-86M (B5, 69.23% F1) by +21.09 percentage points, confirming that higher-order polyadic hyperedges successfully generalize across disparate attack taxonomies without task-specific fine-tuning.

V. Real-World Embedded Boards

Because AHD-Jailbreak requires only 23 MB for an INT8 ONNX encoder and < 15 MB for snapshot \(G_t\), it deploys directly onto physical edge devices without cloud connectivity:

A. Dual-NIC Firewall (Raspberry Pi 5 8GB): Deployed in an in-line "bump-in-the-wire" topology separating untrusted enterprise LAN (eth0) from the protected LLM host (eth1). Leveraging ONNX Runtime ARM64 INT8 quantization and PyG HypergraphConv, the pipeline executes feature extraction (21.0 ms), k-NN ego extraction (2.4 ms), and hypergraph message passing (3.1 ms) in 26.5 ms total median latency (44.8 ms P95) while drawing under 8W of system power and under 650 MB of host RAM.

B. Air-Gapped Robotics (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano 8GB): Running concurrently with a local 4-bit Llama-3.2-3B model via Ollama on a single 15W edge module. TensorRT FP16 hypergraph screening achieves 9.4 ms median latency (18.2 ms P95), intercepting multi-turn Crescendo and indirect sensor prompt injections before actuator actuation.

C. Privacy-Preserving HMAC-SHA256 Telemetry: Fleets operate with LOG_RAW_PROMPTS=false, recording only salted HMAC-SHA256 digests in SQLite WAL mode. Candidate topology updates are evaluated against the locked 500-sample Anchor Validation Gate (Module 14), with automatic rollback upon adversarial poison detection.

D. Edge Resource Footprint & Power Efficiency: On ARM64 Linux, the entire defense runtime—including the quantized INT8 ONNX model, PyG sparse tensors, and cached cosine k-NN index—requires only 642 MB peak resident memory (RSS). Under sustained 100 queries/sec traffic, the Raspberry Pi 5 maintains a thermal envelope below 54°C with passive cooling, consuming 4.2W of additional power over baseline idle. This confirms that AHD-Jailbreak operates strictly within the SWaP (Size, Weight, and Power) constraints of cyber-physical edge appliances.

VI. Conclusion & Limitations

AHD-Jailbreak demonstrates that an inductive, anchor-gated heterogeneous hypergraph effectively bridges the gap between stateless sequence classifiers and heavyweight GPU judge models, intercepting multi-turn and compositional jailbreaks in < 30 ms on commodity CPUs and embedded boards. We note three operational limitations for future work: (1) because all-MiniLM-L6-v2 remains frozen during micro-batch updates, adaptation refines relational topology rather than learning new token semantics; (2) quarantine promotion relies on periodic high-confidence oracle verification; and (3) extending hyperedges to vision/audio modalities will further harden multimodal VLMs.
